Theoretical Limits: Huffman vs. Modern Encoders

Canonical Huffman: Bounded by the whole-integer bit limit (⌈-log2 Pi⌉ ≥ 1 bit per symbol). Even when an instruction opcode occurs 80% of the time, Huffman must allocate at least 1 full bit per occurrence.

Arithmetic & Range Coding: Sub-divides the real interval [0, 1), effectively encoding symbols in fractional bits (log2 1/Pi bits) to match the theoretical Shannon entropy H(X).

Zstandard (Finite State Entropy / tANS): Combines the speed of table-based Huffman state machines with the fractional-bit density of arithmetic coding, making it the premier choice for modern compiler bitstreams.

Compiler Domain Optimization vs. General Compression

General Compressors (zlib / zstd / LZ4): Unaware of programming language semantics; they identify repeating byte subsequences via sliding-window string hashes.

Our SSA Chordal Register Packing: Exploits graph theory (Hack's Theorem) to guarantee virtual variables collapse into k ≤ 16 physical registers, shrinking operand bit-width by 60-75% prior to any byte-level entropy stage.

Our Columnar SoA Container: Separates homogeneous opcodes from variable deltas and symbol strings, maximizing the run-length and entropy predictability for downstream compressors.
